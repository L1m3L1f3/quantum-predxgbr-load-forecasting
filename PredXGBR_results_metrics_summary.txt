Source PDF: electronics-13-04521 (1).pdf
Paper: Zabin, R.; Haque, K.F.; Abdelgawad, A. PredXGBR: A Machine Learning Framework for Short-Term Electrical Load Prediction. Electronics 2024, 13, 4521.
Purpose: Gather all reported result metrics as text for comparison with other research.

============================================================
1. MAIN CLAIMS / OVERALL RESULTS
============================================================
- Proposed model: PredXGBR-1, an XGBoost regression model using short-term lag features.
- Short-term lag features: load mean and standard deviation over previous 6, 12, and 24 hours.
- Long-term lag features used in Model2: aggregate statistics over previous 24 h, 48 h, and weekly intervals.
- Evaluation datasets: PJM, PJME, PJMW, AEP, Dayton.
- Main evaluation metrics: MAPE (%) and R2.
- Reported overall PredXGBR-1 performance: MAPE between 0.98% and 1.28% in Table 3; paper abstract/conclusion summarizes MAPE around 0.98–1.2% and R2 close to 0.99.
- PredXGBR-1 achieved R2 values of 0.98–0.99 across datasets.
- PredXGBR-2 / Model2 performed much worse for XGBoost in Table 3, with R2 between 0.57 and 0.71.

Reported exact values in text:
Model | Lag Type | GFLOPS | Inference Time

TCN-1 | Short-term lag / Model1 | 5.5 GFLOPS | 1.2 ms
SVM-1 | Short-term lag / Model1 | 1.0 GFLOPS | 0.15 ms

Paper interpretation:
- PredXGBR-1 is around 93% faster in inference time than Transformer-2.
- PredXGBR-1 is around 85% less computationally demanding than Transformer-2.
- SVM-1 is the most computationally efficient among the reported short-term models, but PredXGBR-1 gives much better prediction accuracy.
- PredXGBR-1 balances low inference time and high predictive accuracy, making it suitable for real-time short-term load forecasting.

8. GENERALIZATION PERFORMANCE SUMMARY
============================================================
Figure 8 compares generalization performance when models are trained on one dataset and tested on other datasets.
Models compared:
- SVM-1
- TCN-1
- PredXGBR-1

Text-reported findings:
- PredXGBR-1 achieves lower MAPE and higher R2 than SVM-1 and TCN-1 across cross-dataset testing scenarios.
- When trained on PJM, PredXGBR-1 keeps MAPE below 5% across test datasets, while SVM-1 and TCN-1 are often around 7–10%.
- When trained on PJME, PJMW, and AEP, PredXGBR-1 usually records MAPE about 3–5% lower than SVM-1 and TCN-1.
- When trained on AEP, PredXGBR-1 achieves around 1% MAPE on other datasets.
- PredXGBR-1 generally maintains R2 close to or above 0.95 across most test scenarios.
- SVM-1 and TCN-1 show more variable R2 values, sometimes dropping below 0.9.

Important note: Exact numeric values for every Figure 8 bar are not provided in the text extraction; the figure mainly gives visual bar charts. The summary above captures the numeric ranges and statements written in the paper text.

============================================================
9. EVALUATION METRIC DEFINITIONS
============================================================
MAPE = (1/n) * sum(|(y_i - yhat_i) / y_i|) * 100
- Measures average percentage error.
- Lower MAPE means better forecasting accuracy.

R2 = 1 - [sum((y_i - yhat_i)^2) / sum((y_i - ybar)^2)]
- Measures goodness of fit.
- R2 closer to 1 means better fit to actual load values.

10. QUICK COMPARISON TEXT YOU CAN USE IN RESEARCH WRITING
============================================================
The PredXGBR study evaluated short-term electrical load forecasting across five datasets: PJM, PJME, PJMW, AEP, and Dayton. The proposed short-term lag XGBoost model, PredXGBR-1, achieved the best overall performance with MAPE values from 0.98% to 1.28% and R2 values from 0.98 to 0.99. Compared with SVM, RNN, LSTM, TCN, and Transformer baselines, PredXGBR-1 had the lowest average MAPE of 1.104% and the highest average R2 of 0.988. It also maintained low computational cost, requiring 1.5 GFLOPS and 0.2 ms inference time, making it suitable for real-time short-term forecasting.
